# Outcome Delegation

Draft a brief that a recipient can execute and verify without the original conversation. Drafting does not authorize dispatch or execution. Leave the approach to the executor within the brief's boundaries.

## Prepare the brief

- Extract the desired outcome and preserve explicit constraints. Identify requirements separately from preferences; do not turn preferences into requirements.
- Use available context to resolve reversible details. Label material assumptions. Ask only when an unresolved point materially changes the outcome, risk, or authorization; otherwise proceed. If a necessary decision remains open, identify the decision and provide the brief as far as possible.
- Include only essential context and references the recipient can access. Include necessary facts from the original conversation in the brief itself; do not rely on hidden context or inaccessible references.
- Define observable, proportionate evidence of the outcome before work starts. Do not substitute activities, effort, or process for evidence of completion.

Return these five fields:

1. **Outcome:** The deliverable or decision the work must enable, including essential context, requirements, preferences, and material assumptions.
2. **Minimum evidence:** The observable results or artifacts needed to establish that the outcome and its requirements were achieved.
3. **Boundaries and unknowns:** What is out of scope, what may remain unresolved, and what decisions or authorizations belong to the user.
4. **Budget:** Any authorized limits on time, money, resources, or scope. If no limit is given, say so; label any suggested limit as a proposal, not authorization to spend.
5. **Stop condition:** When to deliver, report partial progress, or escalate a specific blocker, including a needed decision or authorization.

## Review a result

Assess the result against the original brief and its minimum evidence, without moving acceptance criteria after work starts. Report one status and the supporting evidence, gaps, and blocker or decision needed, as applicable:

- **delivered:** The outcome and all required evidence are established within the boundaries.
- **partial:** Some useful work or evidence exists, but the outcome or required evidence remains incomplete.
- **blocked:** A specific obstacle prevents further authorized progress; identify what is needed to proceed.

Do not accept a partial result as complete. Any change to the contract requires the user's decision and applies as an explicit revision, not a retroactive redefinition of completion.
